The Summoning Pit · system reference · 31 August 2026

Anatomy of the Pit

One room, one simulation, many watchers. A player types a sentence; a language model composes a body from it; the engine prices that body so it cannot be unfair; and the creature walks into a persistent world that every other player is watching at the same moment. Nothing is drawn by hand and nothing is picked from a list.

01 — the whole loop

Four domains, one authority

The server owns the world outright. No client predicts, nothing rolls back, and nobody drives a character — creatures decide for themselves — so the hard parts of netcode do not apply here. Only two things cross the wire: positions at a fixed rate, and events the instant they happen. Genomes travel once, by index, because the expensive data is the only part that never changes.

02 — a summoning, in order

From sentence to standing creature

The only genuine sequence in the system. Everything else is a loop or a lattice; this runs once, in this order, in 7 to 19 seconds.

  1. The words leave, and are never written down

    The client sends the sentence and its key over the socket. The prompt is held in memory for the length of the request and released. It is never written to disk, never logged, and never appears in a creature's name or on the wire.

    t:'summon'≤200 chars2 s cooldown

  2. Two bodies are composed in parallel

    The same sentence goes twice to the 70B at different temperatures, against a JSON schema describing a skeleton, its chains, gait, palette, what it holds, what it wears, and how it attacks. Two samples, because one model call is a coin toss and the pit would rather choose.

    Llama-3.3-70Bresponse_format: jsonmax_tokens 3400

  3. The first to arrive sets the pace

    Waiting for both meant the slowest sample decided the wait — 21 seconds against 7, measured. The first body to land starts the clock; its sibling gets a 5-second grace to arrive and earn a comparison. Whatever has not happened by then does not happen.

    GRACE_MS 5000

  4. A judge looks at both and keeps one

    Both candidates are posed and rendered to 224px, and a vision model is asked which reads as a single coherent creature rather than scattered shapes. It is a picker, not a gate: it ranks two images and never rejects. If it does not answer within 9 seconds, a deterministic anatomy check — connectivity, grounding, stray parts — decides instead, in under 5 ms.

    Qwen3.8-Flashthinking disabledEYE_MS 9000

  5. The engine prices what the model imagined

    Fairness is never a whitelist. A weapon's volume is capped, tempo × reach is capped so a fast far attack stretches its own duration back out, a projectile's speed × presence × blast is capped, and total mass is squeezed into a band. Ambition costs character, not damage.

    volume ≤ 0.012tempo×reach ≤ 3.2shot ≤ 36

  6. It is sanitised, named, and it walks in

    Every numeric field is clamped and every enum whitelisted on the server — the model's output is treated as hostile input, because it is input. The creature is named from its own skeleton, given a cast index, spawned, and appears in the next snapshot on every screen watching.

    one hero per ownernext snapshot ≤ 67 ms

03 — the body

Genome, skeleton, capsule field

Composition

A creature is a genome: a spine of nodes, a set of chains hung off it by role (arm, leg, tail, wing, spike, tentacle), a gait, a palette, and its gear. Nothing is a mesh. Every part is a capsule — two points and a radius — which is why a body can be composed by a language model in a few hundred numbers and still pose, fight and render.

Pose

Limbs solve by two-bone IK against a target and a pole vector. Legs cycle on distance travelled, not on a clock: one stride is one cycle whatever the speed, so backing off runs the cycle in reverse and strafing still lifts the feet. A clock-driven cycle is what makes a creature look like it is skating.

Secondary motion

The parts that arrive late are springs, not animation: the bank that overshoots a turn, the torso dragged round after the feet, weight landing, flesh still moving after the frame stopped, and the head — which arrives last and overshoots when it does. This is most of what separates "animated" from "alive".

Idle

Standing still is three sines of idle time — breath at 0.35 Hz, a sway at 0.6, a limb drift at 0.3. Their periods are 20⁄7, 5⁄3 and 10⁄3 seconds and first coincide at exactly 20 seconds, which is the only duration at which an idle loop closes without a visible jump.

04 — the picture

A field, marched per pixel

There is no polygon anywhere in the renderer. For every pixel it marches the capsule field and resolves the nearest surface, then fuses neighbours with an exponential smooth-minimum so silhouettes melt at the joints and each part's ink cross-fades into the next. Bodies read as one mass rather than an assembly of sticks. Outlines are added afterwards: silhouette edges darkened, and interior edges wherever a nearer limb sits in front of the one behind it.

05 — the wire

Positions at a rate, events at once

Snapshots leave every second tick — counted in ticks, not hertz, because a snapshot can only depart on a tick boundary and a rate that does not divide the tick grid is a rate the server never achieves. Events do not wait for the next position packet: they carry the story, they are sparse, and they go the instant they happen.

The playback clock

Clients render the world 0.45 of a snapshot gap in the past — a fraction, never a fixed number of seconds. A fixed delay larger than the gap aims the clock before the previous snapshot, pins interpolation at zero, and the world moves at the snapshot rate while the phone draws at 60: the exact signature of "low frame rate" that no amount of rendering work can fix. Playback runs on the server's own clock rather than packet arrival times, so network jitter never becomes a change in how fast the world appears to move.

06 — the fight

A triangle, and a cost for winning

The triangle

Light beats guard-break. Guard beats light. Heavy and feint beat guard. Interrupt beats a heavy still winding up. Every exchange resolves through this, not through a damage roll: a clean block staggers the attacker and opens a riposte; a parry on a bare weapon smashes it aside; a feint beats a guard six times in ten.

Where the blow lands

A strike picks a spot — high, middle or low, left or right — and the arm solves to it, so the animation and the hit are the same fact. A feint shows the opposite line and swaps at the end of the wind-up. The choice comes from a seed both server and client compute identically, so every screen sees the same swing.

The crown gets heavy

A long reign used to compound: every kill grew the lord, every quiet minute healed it to full. Now, after the first free hour and first free kill, every second trophy and every hour held is a scar — one point of health no rest, and no blood-won surge, ever restores. Capped at 45% of the bar: worn down, not hollowed out.

scars = min(⌊0.45·maxHp⌋, ⌊kills/2 + (hours − 1)⌋)

The floor remembers

Bones and dropped arms stay where they fell, get kicked around, and sink. A thrown spear becomes a relic its owner will walk back to collect. Plants grow, get trampled, and reseed. All of it is server-authoritative and persisted, so the pit you return to is the pit you left.

08 — invariants

Rules the system will not break

Prompts are never stored, logged, or exposed. Held in memory for one request. A weapon's fallback name is drawn from a matched vocabulary noun, never a slice of what someone typed, because a name travels the wire and sits in the save.
The model composes; the engine budgets. Invention is unbounded and power is priced. No whitelist of allowed creatures has ever existed, and adding one would end the game.
The server is the only authority. Every model output is treated as hostile input and clamped on arrival. Clients render; they never decide.
Identity survives the network. A phone that locks, changes network or backgrounds drops its socket silently. The newest socket holds the key, so a reconnect is the same person — never a stranger handed a new name.
A judge ranks; it never rejects. Taste chooses between two candidates that both exist. There is always a creature to show, and the summoner never waits on an opinion for longer than nine seconds.
